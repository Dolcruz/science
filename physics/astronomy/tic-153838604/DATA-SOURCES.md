# Data sources and acknowledgments

## Candidate identification

Faith Hawthorn and colleagues identified TIC 153838604 as a duotransit candidate and published its first two events and period aliases in 2024. The later 2025 TESS events supply the additional timing measurements in this analysis. The candidate identifier and public follow-up inventory were checked through the Exoplanet Follow-up Observing Program (ExoFOP), operated by Caltech for NASA’s Exoplanet Exploration Program. [Hawthorn et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stad3783) · [ExoFOP, DOI 10.26134/ExoFOP5](https://doi.org/10.26134/ExoFOP5)

## TESS light curves and images

NASA’s Science Mission Directorate funds TESS. The TESS Science Office at MIT produced the Quick-Look Pipeline (QLP) light curves, distributed by the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST) at the Space Telescope Science Institute. Huang et al. (2020a, 2020b) and Kunimoto et al. (2021, 2022) describe the products. The sector 97 and 98 reductions use the TESS-Gaia Light Curve method of Han & Brandt (2023). MAST distributes the QLP products under CC BY 4.0. The eclipse fits and timing tables are derived from those light curves. [QLP dataset, DOI 10.17909/t9-r086-e880](https://doi.org/10.17909/t9-r086-e880) · [MIT pipeline and methodology](https://tess.mit.edu/qlp/) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The aperture comparisons use the TESS Science Processing Operations Center’s TESS-SPOC high-level products (Caldwell et al. 2020), distributed by MAST under CC BY 4.0. The pixel analysis uses MAST TESScut (Brasseur et al. 2019) to extract time series from the TESS full-frame images. The difference images combine frames inside and outside eclipse. [TESS-SPOC dataset, DOI 10.17909/t9-wpz1-8s54](https://doi.org/10.17909/t9-wpz1-8s54) · [MAST TESScut](https://mast.stsci.edu/tesscut/)

## Ground-based light curves

The All-Sky Automated Survey for Supernovae (ASAS-SN) supplied Sky Patrol V2 target and comparison-star photometry and legacy V-band photometry. These data underlie the matched-night and differential measurements. The survey and its light-curve services are described by Shappee et al. (2014), Kochanek et al. (2017) and Hart et al. (2023). [ASAS-SN survey and citation guidance](https://www.astronomy.ohio-state.edu/asassn/public/) · [Kochanek et al.](https://doi.org/10.1088/1538-3873/aa80d9) · [Hart et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03791)

The NASA Exoplanet Archive makes the KELT-South raw and trend-filtered light curves available on behalf of the Kilodegree Extremely Little Telescope project team. Caltech operates the archive for NASA. [KELT archive and references](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/KELT.html) · [KELT dataset, DOI 10.26133/NEA8](https://doi.org/10.26133/NEA8)

## Astrometry, spectroscopy and field imaging

The European Space Agency’s Gaia mission supplied the DR3 measurements. The Gaia Data Processing and Analysis Consortium (DPAC) processed the observations with support from national institutions, especially the participants in the Gaia Multilateral Agreement. The spatially varying astrometric quality threshold comes from Castro-Ginard et al. (2024) and their GaiaUnlimited release. [Gaia Collaboration, DR3](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202243940) · [Gaia/DPAC credits](https://gaia.aip.de/cms/credit/) · [GaiaUnlimited threshold data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.11102437)

The J and Ks cutouts are based on VISTA Hemisphere Survey (VHS) observations made with VIRCAM at the European Southern Observatory under programme 179.A-2010(G). The ESO Science Archive Facility supplied the processed images. The field figure displays cropped survey data. The archive data are © ESO and distributed under CC BY 4.0. [VHS collection, DOI 10.18727/archive/57](https://doi.org/10.18727/archive/57) · [ESO data policy](https://archive.eso.org/cms/eso-data-access-policy.html)

Catalog checks used SIMBAD and VizieR at the Centre de données astronomiques de Strasbourg (CDS), including the VHS DR5 and Dark Energy Survey DR2 catalogs. [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/) · [VizieR](https://doi.org/10.26093/cds/vizier) · [Dark Energy Survey Collaboration, DR2](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/ac00b3)

## Infrared photometry

The NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA), operated by Caltech with NASA funding, supplied the AllWISE and NEOWISE single-exposure photometry, AllWISE source photometry and 2MASS source catalog. WISE and the NEOWISE reactivation mission were joint projects of UCLA and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory/Caltech, funded by NASA. AllWISE also incorporates the original NEOWISE project at JPL/Caltech. The University of Massachusetts and IPAC/Caltech carried out 2MASS with support from NASA and the National Science Foundation. [IRSA and 2MASS credits](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/knowledgebase/general.html) · [AllWISE acknowledgments](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/AllWISE/expsup/sec1_6b.html) · [NEOWISE final release](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/NEOWISE/)
