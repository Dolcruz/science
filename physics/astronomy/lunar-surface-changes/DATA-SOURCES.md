# Data sources and attribution

## Orbital images and maps

**Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera image credit: NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University.** The Narrow Angle Camera images come from the NASA Planetary Data System (PDS). The Geosciences Node’s Orbital Data Explorer supplies observation geometry and acquisition metadata. The LROC team identifies products distributed through the PDS as public domain. The field directories give individual product IDs. The image cutouts have been cropped and registered to local terrain. Display panels apply the brightness normalization or contrast stretch described for each field. [Calibrated Data Record](https://doi.org/10.17189/1520241) · [Robinson et al. (2010), instrument](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-010-9634-2) · [Humm et al. (2016), NAC calibration](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-015-0201-8) · [Speyerer et al. (2012), geometric calibration](https://doi.org/10.5194/isprsarchives-XXXIX-B4-511-2012) · [LROC terms and citation policy](https://www.lroc.im-ldi.com/about/terms)

The N13 position check uses a Lunaserv web map. The archived request identifies the image to retrieve, and the terrain-match table reports the resulting measurements. The LROC team requires prior permission for commercial use of images downloaded outside its PDS archive. [Map request](reconstructed/wms/wms.url) · [Estes et al. (2013), Lunaserv](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2013/pdf/2609.pdf) · [Robinson (2010), LROC Experimental Data Record](https://doi.org/10.17189/1520250) · [LROC terms](https://www.lroc.im-ldi.com/about/terms)

The SELENE (Kaguya) Terrain Camera team and the SELENE Data Archive at DARTS, ISAS/JAXA supplied morning and evening reflectance mosaics for a separate N13 position check. JAXA developed and operated the mission. The morning mosaic yielded the reported terrain matches. [Morning mosaic](https://darts.isas.jaxa.jp/en/datasets/darts:sln-l-tc-5-morning-map-v4.0/) · [Evening mosaic](https://darts.isas.jaxa.jp/en/datasets/darts:sln-l-tc-5-evening-map-v4.0/) · [Kaguya data-use policy](https://darts.isas.jaxa.jp/missions/kaguya/faq.html)

## Published maps and catalogs

- **McGetchin terrain and Figure 7.** Mark S. Robinson and colleagues (2026) released calibrated images, orthophotos and aligned terrain models for *A New 222-m Diameter Lunar Crater* under CC BY 4.0. The local cutouts, stereo profiles and height residuals use those data. The annotated map in the report and in `mcgetchin/published-map.png` adapts Figure 7 (© 2026 the authors, CC BY 4.0) by cropping it and adding site markers. [Robinson et al., data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21481275) · [Original Figure 7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13580579/#F7) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
- **Crater and cold-spot positions.** The comparison table combines the LROC new-crater shapefile with the crater, unresolved-position and surface-marking tables of Aleksandra Sokołowska and colleagues (2025), and the global cold-spot and new-crater tables of Tyler Powell and colleagues (2026). Both Zenodo data sets carry CC BY 4.0. Selected identifiers, coordinates and diameters were brought into a common east-longitude table, then used to calculate great-circle distances. The source manifest gives the original files and their SHA-256 hashes. [PDS shapefile](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/view_rdr/SHAPEFILE_GLOBAL_NEW_CRATERS) · [Sokołowska et al., data and 16th Planetary Crater Consortium abstract citation](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15755155) · [Powell et al., data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19472800) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) · [Source manifest](catalog/sources.csv)
- **Original impact identifications.** The reconstructed catalog draws on temporal crater mapping by Speyerer et al. (2016), imaging of the March 2013 impact and its secondary markings by Robinson et al. (2015), and earlier impact monitoring summarized by Oberst et al. (2012). [Speyerer et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature19829) · [Robinson et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2015.01.019) · [Oberst et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2012.10.005)

## Ground-based flashes and spacecraft chronology

Liakos et al. (2024) published the NELIOTA observations used to select image fields and check event times. The CSV files extract dates, classifications, positions and estimated crater diameters from the paper, with east longitudes and site distances calculated for the comparisons. The paper is licensed under CC BY 4.0. Its catalog is also available through VizieR at the Centre de Données astronomiques de Strasbourg (CDS). [Liakos et al., A&A 687, A14](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202449542) · [VizieR J/A+A/687/A14](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/ReadMe/J/A%2BA/687/A14?format=html) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The NELIOTA project developed the Lunar Impact Flashes Database. ESA funded the first two phases. The European Union’s Horizon Europe Programme funded the third phase, implemented by ESA. The observations used the 1.2 m Kryoneri telescope of the National Observatory of Athens, operated by its Institute for Astronomy, Astrophysics, Space Applications and Remote Sensing. The online cross-check records dates, times, observer credits and links to individual public event records. The LROC anthropogenic-objects catalog and NASA’s spacecraft chronology supply the comparison dates for known hardware impacts. [Lunar Impact Flashes Database](https://neliota.astro.noa.gr/LIFDatabase) · [LROC anthropogenic objects](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/view_rdr/SHAPEFILE_ANTHROPOGENIC_OBJECTS) · [NASA lunar impact chronology](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/lunar_artifact_impacts.html)
