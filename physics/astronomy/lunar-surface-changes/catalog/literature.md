# Published coverage of the northwestern McGetchin site

**The candidate near 1.36151° N, 67.16426° E lies inside the discovery team's published imagery.** Robinson et al. imaged the terrain before and after the 2024 impact in Figure 1. Their 950 m-wide Figure 7 distinguishes ejected boulders from craters that already existed before the impact. Supplementary Figures S1 and S2 map topography and boulders, S3 maps pre-impact craters larger than 6 m, and S4 compares terrain models. None gives an identification or coordinates for a new secondary crater at this northwestern site. The accompanying archive contains NAC images, orthophotos and terrain models cropped around the primary. Its published files include no point catalog of new secondaries. [Discovery paper, Figures 1 and 7](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeh7812) · [Supplementary Figures S1–S4](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13580579.1/sciadv.aeh7812_sm.pdf) · [Image and terrain archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21481275)

A literature and citation search through **20 November 2026** found no public paper or catalog assigning this position to a secondary. The companion Diviner study reports regional regolith changes and lists 21 newly formed craters larger than 20 m. An October comparison discusses the primary crater's depth. Neither supplies a meter-scale secondary-crater inventory. The evidence supports describing the hollow as a **candidate secondary in previously published imagery**. The available record leaves its discovery priority and connection to ejecta from McGetchin open.
